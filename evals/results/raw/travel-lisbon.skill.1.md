# Why Lisbon, Why Now

Lisbon rewards a closer look: coastal, layered with history, good light, good food. A long weekend here is enough to notice how the old city and the new one sit next to each other without much friction.

## Arrival and First Impressions

Landing in Lisbon, what hits you first is the light, then the trams grinding uphill, then how unhurried everyone seems. The hills aren't just geography — they slow you down and force a different angle on the city than you'd get walking flat streets.

## A City Built on Contrasts

The Alfama's narrow, winding streets sit close to the sleek riverside developments nearby, and neither seems to be trying to win. Walk from a centuries-old miradouro to a contemporary gallery and the craft on display is the same kind of attention, just aimed at different centuries.

## Food, Culture, and Connection

The food follows the same logic: simple ingredients, careful preparation, hospitality that doesn't feel performed. A meal here is as much about sitting still for an hour as it is about what's on the plate. Music and art and the ordinary noise of the street fill in the rest.

## Making the Most of Limited Time

With only a weekend, you have to choose. The visits that work best mix the landmarks everyone comes for with a neighborhood or two and enough unscheduled time to just wander — skip the wandering and you'll leave with a checklist instead of a memory of the place.

Lisbon is worth keeping on the list.
