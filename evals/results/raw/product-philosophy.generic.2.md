# The Great Rebuild: Why We Reimagined Our Product From the Ground Up

Our engineering team made a decision that put us in the uncomfortable position of rebuilding something users already depended on: rather than keep patching what we had, we tore it down and built again from the ground up.

## Listening Before Building

We spent months gathering feedback from customers, partners, and internal stakeholders before writing a line of code. We didn't just log feature requests — we tried to understand the reasoning behind them. What came back consistently was that people didn't want another round of small fixes. They wanted a foundation that could support where the product was going, not just where it had been.

## Three Principles

The new architecture rests on three ideas: simplicity, scalability, and sustainability. Simplicity means complexity stays out of the way of the experience. Scalability means the system is built for growth we haven't hit yet, not just the load we see today. Sustainability means the engineering practices behind it are meant to hold up over time, not require another rebuild in a few years. The old system made users adapt to it. This one is built to adapt to them.

## Why Rebuild Instead of Patch

We could have kept layering features onto the old foundation. We chose not to, because doing so would have meant carrying forward assumptions and legacy decisions we no longer agreed with. Starting over let us question those assumptions directly and design with intention instead of working around what was already there.

## A Cross-Functional Effort

No single team did this. Engineers, designers, and product strategists worked from a shared vision, and the collaboration between them mattered as much as any individual decision made along the way. Where the old process ran into silos, this one didn't.

The rebuild is done, but it's really a starting point — a foundation we intend to keep building on with the people who use the product every day.
