Barack Obama was born in Honolulu, Hawaii in 1961, to a Kenyan father and an American mother. He worked as a community organizer in Chicago, went through Harvard Law School, and in 2008 became the first African American President of the United States.

Over two terms, his administration pursued initiatives on healthcare, the economy, and foreign policy, each of which sparked significant debate. He was also known for his oratory skill, treating public communication as central to the job rather than incidental to it.

Since leaving office, Obama has continued writing, speaking, and engaging publicly on issues he cares about, without holding formal office. His path — community organizer, president, public figure — remains a reference point in discussions of American leadership and political change.
