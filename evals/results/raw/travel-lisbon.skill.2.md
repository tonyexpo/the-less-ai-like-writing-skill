# Why Lisbon, Why Now

Lisbon works for a long weekend because it doesn't ask you to choose: coastal, historic, good light, good food — you get all of it without much effort. Tradition and modernity aren't at odds here. They share a street.

## Arrival and First Impressions

Land in Lisbon and the light gets you first, then the trams grinding up the hills, then how unhurried people are with you. The hills themselves are the city's real trick — you can't rush them, so you stop trying and start looking around instead.

## A City Built on Contrasts

The Alfama's narrow, winding streets sit a few minutes' walk from glass-fronted riverside developments, and neither seems to mind the other. Centuries-old miradouros and contemporary galleries share the same throughline: craft taken seriously, whatever form it's in.

## Food, Culture, and Connection

The food follows the same logic — simple ingredients, careful preparation, no rush to clear the table. A meal here is time spent with someone, not just a meal. Music, art, and the ordinary traffic of the street round out the rest of it.

## Making the Most of Limited Time

With only a weekend, the trick is balance: see the landmarks, walk a real neighborhood, and leave hours with nothing planned. Skip the wandering and you'll remember the sights but not the city. Overschedule and you won't have room for either.

Lisbon rewards exactly this kind of visit — short, a little loose, and open to whatever the hills put in front of you.
