# Migration Moment: Why the Postgres 12 → 16 Upgrade Matters Now

In today's fast-moving data landscape, the database layer isn't just infrastructure—it's the foundation that everything else stands on. As our systems scale, staying current with the platforms that power them isn't optional; it's essential. That's why, on the 14th, we're moving from Postgres 12 to Postgres 16.

## What's Happening

**The upgrade date: the 14th.** This isn't just a date on a calendar—it's a milestone in our ongoing commitment to modern infrastructure. Marking it now means less friction later.

**The write freeze: roughly 20 minutes.** Short, deliberate, and scoped. It's not downtime for downtime's sake—it's a brief pause that makes a smoother upgrade possible. Whether you're pushing a hotfix or wrapping up a feature branch, a few minutes of planning now saves headaches later.

**The read replicas: staying up.** While writes pause, reads carry on. It's a small distinction with a real impact: dashboards, reports, and read-heavy services can keep humming along largely undisturbed.

## Why This Approach

Three things define this migration: **minimal disruption**, **clear communication**, and **forward compatibility**. Together, they reflect a simple philosophy—upgrade boldly, but responsibly.

It's not just about moving to a newer version. It's about making sure that progress and stability aren't a trade-off but a package deal.

## Looking Ahead

Migrations like this one are part of a broader story—one where our systems don't just keep pace with change, but stay ready for whatever comes next. The 14th is a step, not a finish line, and how we prepare for it says a lot about how we build.

As always, more details will follow as the date approaches. Thanks, as ever, for adapting alongside the systems we all depend on.
